PROVENANCE

Following the sand
across Baltica

PAPER2023 · PRECAMBRIAN RESEARCH
MY ROLELEAD AUTHOR
READ TIME~3 MINUTES
FORMATPLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY

Zircon ages from drill cores in Estonia and the Volyn region of Ukraine show Baltica's margins switching from quiet passive margins to collision zones.

Sand grains carry a memory of the rocks they eroded from. Among them, tiny crystals of zircon are especially useful: they survive transport and recycling, and each one can be dated. By comparing the ages of many detrital zircons, we can tell which mountain belts, shields or volcanic arcs were supplying sediment to a basin — and how that changed over time.

In this study we used detrital zircon ages to reconstruct how the late Ediacaran to early Cambrian sedimentary basins of northwestern, western and southwestern Baltica evolved.

Samples and approach

We dated zircons by U–Pb LA-ICP-MS from three sandstone samples from a drill core in northeastern Estonia and four sandstone samples from three drill cores in the Volyn region of Ukraine. We then compared the resulting age patterns with published data from southwestern Ukraine and Moldova, eastern Poland, Belarus and northwestern Russia.

FIG. 1 — Simplified sketch of Baltica in modern coordinates, showing the two new sampling areas (rust) and the regions used for comparison. Orogens grew along the south-western and north-eastern margins during the late Ediacaran–early Cambrian. Not to scale.

The big picture

All of these basins record the same overall transition: from a passive continental margin to collisional settings. The change was driven mainly by the pre-Scythides and Santacrusades orogenies along the south-southwestern margin of Baltica and, to a lesser extent, by the Timanian Orogeny along the north-northeastern and eastern margins (in modern coordinates).

Region by region

Southwestern Baltica (Moldova-Podillya-Volyn). The basin started on a flattened Precambrian basement in the distal part of a foreland basin, fed steadily by the nearby Ukrainian Shield. Later in the Redkino stage, volcanic ash arrived from arcs close to — but separate from — Baltica. As the forebulge migrated, the basin briefly emerged, then subsided again into a foredeep in the Kotlin stage. The sediment supply shifted back and forth between the Ukrainian Shield and Fennoscandia.

Northwestern Russia. Sediment first came from Fennoscandia; from the Dominopol stage onward, the rising Timanian Orogen also fed the St Petersburg and White Sea areas.

Eastern Poland and Belarus. A bimodal zircon age pattern reflects short-distance transport from the local basement. It became polymodal during the Dominopol stage, when major tectonic changes reorganised the drainage system.

Estonia. The most stable record of all. From the Kotlin to the early Cambrian Lontova horizons, sediment came almost entirely from the Precambrian basement — the Svecofennian orogen (ca. 1900–1800 Ma) and ca. 1700–1500 Ma rapakivi-type intrusions of southwestern Fennoscandia. The source stayed the same for nearly 50 million years, until new magmatic-arc sources appeared in the upper Dominopol horizon.

Why it matters

Baltica was the core of what became Europe. Knowing when and how its quiet margins turned into active, collisional ones helps place the Ediacaran–Cambrian sedimentary record — and the fossils it contains — in its tectonic context, and makes correlation between basins across the continent far more reliable.

KEY TERMS
Detrital zirconDurable zircon grains eroded from older rocks; their ages fingerprint the source area.
Passive marginA continental edge without an active plate boundary, with slow subsidence and steady sedimentation.
Foreland basinA basin that forms in front of a growing mountain belt as the crust is loaded and flexed.
ForebulgeA gentle upward flexure of the crust ahead of a foreland basin; it can lift a basin above sea level as it migrates.
Regional horizonsLocal stratigraphic units of the East European Platform (Redkino, Kotlin, Rivne, Dominopol, Lontova…).
