GEOCHRONOLOGY

Ice or rivers?
Testing a disputed
glacial deposit with zircon

PAPER2024 · PRECAMBRIAN RESEARCH
MY ROLELEAD AUTHOR
READ TIME~3 MINUTES
FORMATPLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY

Were the Bakhtyn Beds diamictites left by glaciers? Detrital zircon ages say the sediment came from the local basement — pointing to rivers, not ice.

The Neoproterozoic is famous for its “Snowball Earth” glaciations: the Sturtian (ca. 715–660 Ma) and the Marinoan (ca. 639–635 Ma), followed by the shorter and possibly regional Gaskiers glaciation at ca. 580–579 Ma. The rest of the Ediacaran and the early Cambrian are usually seen as warm, without glaciers reaching sea level at low latitudes.

Yet around the world, a number of local or regional sedimentary units from this warm interval have been interpreted as glacial deposits. Many of them crop out poorly, have been deformed or metamorphosed, lack diagnostic sedimentary structures, and are poorly dated. That makes it very hard to decide whether they really record ice.

The Bakhtyn Beds problem

In the Moldova-Podillya basin, the Bakhtyn Beds of the Hrushka Formation (Volyn Group) contain thin, poorly exposed diamictites: massive, poorly sorted coarse sandstones, conglomerates and breccias, with poorly rounded clasts in a fine-grained matrix. Rocks like this can form under a glacier — or in alluvial fans and rivers. Without conclusive glacial features, the debate has continued for a long time.

A provenance test

Our approach was to ask where the sediment came from. Glaciers scrape and mix debris from large, heterogeneous areas, so glacial deposits tend to contain zircons of many different ages. Rivers and alluvial fans usually carry more homogeneous, local material.

FIG. 1 — The logic of the test. A glacier gathers debris from a wide and varied area, producing a broad mix of zircon ages; a local river system delivers zircons mostly from the basement it cuts into. The Bakhtyn Beds match the local pattern. Schematic.

We dated detrital zircons by U–Pb LA-ICP-MS from two sandstones and one conglomerate of the Bakhtyn Beds, and from two sandstones of the unconformably overlying Lomoziv Beds (Mohyliv Formation). We compared them with published data for the overlying Cosaui (Yampil) and Bernashivka beds, and with new dates from the Palaeoproterozoic granites directly underneath, exposed in the Vyshcheolchedaiv and Novodnistrovsk open pits. Drill-core data helped map how far the Volyn Group sediments extend.

What the zircons say

  • The Bakhtyn, Lomoziv and Cosaui/Yampil samples are dominated by Palaeoproterozoic and Meso- to Neoarchean zircon ages — a uniform provenance from the Precambrian basement.
  • Multidimensional scaling with Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistics shows these units are statistically very similar to one another.
  • U–Pb ages and hafnium isotopes of the Bakhtyn zircons match the granites immediately below.
  • The zircons are euhedral to subhedral and not rounded — they have not travelled far.

A minor contribution from more regional basement sources is likely, given the spread of Archean ages. But overall the evidence is consistent with an alluvial, not glacial, origin for the Bakhtyn Beds, deposited under passive-margin conditions that lasted until the lower Bernashivka Beds.

Why it matters

Deciding whether a diamictite is glacial changes how we read past climate. Detrital zircon geochronology offers a largely untapped way to test glacial interpretations for units that are poorly exposed or poorly dated — especially where they sit on a uniform igneous or meta-igneous basement. The same approach can be applied to other disputed “glacial” units worldwide.

KEY TERMS
DiamictiteA poorly sorted rock with large clasts scattered in a fine matrix; can be glacial or non-glacial.
Detrital zirconDurable zircon grains eroded from older rocks; their ages fingerprint where sediment came from.
U–Pb LA-ICP-MSLaser-ablation mass spectrometry used to date individual zircon grains by uranium–lead decay.
ProvenanceThe source area of a sediment.
AlluvialDeposited by rivers and alluvial fans rather than by ice or the sea.
