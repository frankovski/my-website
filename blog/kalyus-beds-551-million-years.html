GEOCHRONOLOGY

Putting a date on
the Kalyus Beds

PAPER2021 · PRECAMBRIAN RESEARCH
MY ROLELEAD AUTHOR
READ TIME~3 MINUTES
FORMATPLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY

The youngest zircons in the Kalyus black argillites date them to about 551 million years — calibrating the Ediacaran biota of the Moldova-Podillya Basin.

The Moldova-Podillya Basin is remarkable for its Ediacaran fossils. But to compare them with fossils elsewhere, we need to know when the rocks were deposited. This paper provides a new age for one of the key units: the Kalyus Beds of the Nagoryany Formation in northeastern Moldova.

The Kalyus Beds are black argillites rich in organic matter, with phosphorite and calcareous concretions. They record low-oxygen (anoxic) conditions on the sea floor, and near their base they contain layers of volcanic tuffite.

Dating the sediment

We dated detrital zircons from the argillites by U–Pb LA-ICP-MS. The ages fall into four clusters: about 1945, 1780, 1525 and 551 Ma.

FIG. 1 — Schematic distribution of detrital zircon ages in the Kalyus Beds. Three older populations come from the Proterozoic crust of the East European Craton; the youngest, ca. 551 Ma, comes from contemporaneous volcanism. Peak heights are illustrative.

The three older clusters match known rock units of the East European Platform: their hafnium isotope values point to distal sources in the Proterozoic crust of the craton. The youngest cluster, 551.2 ± 4.2 Ma, is different. These zircons can be linked to arc-related volcanism — the same volcanism that delivered ash to the tuffite layers. Hafnium isotopes of two of these young zircons suggest a mantle contribution; a third came from felsic magma formed by re-melting older crust.

Because the young zircons were incorporated directly from volcanic ash, or recycled very shortly after eruption, 551 Ma is either the true depositional age or very close to it. Two bentonite (volcanic ash) beds lower in the Podillya succession have independently been dated at 555.4 ± 2.9 Ma and 556.78 ± 0.18 Ma, so the three ages line up in the right order going up-section.

Where did the volcanism come from?

The ash was probably produced by arc volcanism at an active margin adjacent to Baltica, related to the closure of the Thor Ocean and the docking of the Teisseyre-Tornquist Terrane Assemblage to Baltica, which began in the late Ediacaran.

Why it matters

The new age helps calibrate sedimentary and volcanic events in the basin and the evolution of its Ediacaran biota. Macro- and microfossils of the Nagoryany Formation, which belong to the Miaohe biota biozone, can be confined to the interval between about 551.2 and 552.82 Ma. Newly identified taxa also significantly increase the known diversity of that biozone.

The age also allows a correlation with an anoxic episode in the upper Doushantuo Formation of South China. That growing body of evidence suggests that late Ediacaran anoxia may have been more than a local phenomenon — and that such conditions may have contributed to the decline of the Ediacaran fauna.

KEY TERMS
Maximum depositional ageThe age of the youngest zircons in a sediment; the rock cannot be older than this.
TuffiteA rock made of volcanic ash mixed with ordinary sediment.
Hf isotopes (εHf)Tell whether the magma that formed a zircon came from the mantle or from re-melted older crust.
Miaohe biotaA late Ediacaran assemblage of carbonaceous fossils, first described from South China.
AnoxiaAbsence of dissolved oxygen in seawater or pore water.
