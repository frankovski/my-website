GEOCHEMISTRY

Rare earth elements
in the Kalyus Beds

PAPER2020 · GEOCHEMISTRY (CHEMIE DER ERDE)
MY ROLELEAD AUTHOR
READ TIME~3 MINUTES
FORMATPLAIN-LANGUAGE SUMMARY

Phosphorites and black argillites of the Kalyus Beds hold 793–1735 ppm rare earth elements — taken up during diagenesis, and potentially of economic interest.

The Kalyus Beds of the late Ediacaran Nagoryany Formation crop out in northeastern Moldova. They are dominated by black argillites, with phosphorites and calcareous concretions. In this paper we looked at their rare earth element (REE), major- and trace-element geochemistry — both to understand the ancient seawater they formed in and to assess whether they contain economically interesting concentrations of REE.

Four kinds of rock, three REE stories

We grouped the samples into four lithologies: phosphorites, enriched argillites, pure argillites and carbonates. Their REE patterns fall into three distinct shapes.

FIG. 1 — The three pattern shapes found in the Kalyus Beds (normalised REE, schematic): a middle-REE bulge in phosphorites and enriched argillites, a flat crust-like pattern in pure argillites, and a light-REE-depleted, seawater-like pattern in carbonates.
  • Phosphorites and enriched argillites show a pronounced middle-REE bulge — the fingerprint of strong REE uptake during diagenesis. Their REE track phosphorus closely, so the enrichment is controlled by apatite. Y/Ho ratios mostly below 35 indicate that more than 90% of the REE came from land-derived (lithogenous) sources.
  • Pure argillites have flat patterns similar to the average upper continental crust. Their REE sit in detrital clay and silt eroded from felsic to mafic rocks of neighbouring Baltica — plausibly the Ukrainian Shield and the Volyn-Brest flood-basalt province.
  • Carbonates show light-REE depletion and Y/Ho ratios up to 53 — they preserve a mainly seawater-derived signal.

A window of low oxygen

Cerium behaves differently from the other REE when oxygen is present, so the cerium anomaly (Ce/Ce*) is a useful redox indicator. Values of 0.80–1.02 point to suboxic to anoxic seawater while the Kalyus Beds were deposited. Because oxic conditions were widespread on the shallow margins of Baltica at that time, we interpreted this as a local and temporary anoxic episode; how it relates to other late Ediacaran anoxic events deserved further study (and was followed up in our 2021 paper on the age of the Kalyus Beds).

A potential resource

Total REE contents in the phosphorites and enriched argillites are high — 793 to 1735 ppm, with a particular enrichment in the middle REE — and comparable to other phosphorite deposits around the world. The host rock contains less (198–296 ppm), but whole-rock open-pit extraction might still be feasible. More work is needed to identify economically viable areas.

Why it matters

As demand for rare earth elements grows each year, sedimentary phosphorites are attracting attention as alternative sources. The Kalyus Beds show that the Ediacaran succession of Moldova is worth a closer look — both as an archive of ancient ocean chemistry and as a possible exploration target.

KEY TERMS
REE patternConcentrations of the rare earth elements, normalised to a reference, plotted in atomic order; its shape reveals sources and processes.
Ce anomalyCerium depleted relative to its neighbours; a sign of oxygenated water. Values near 1 suggest low oxygen.
Y/Ho ratioSeawater has high Y/Ho; values near crustal ratios point to land-derived material.
Diagenetic uptakeIncorporation of elements into minerals after deposition, from pore waters.
ArgilliteA hardened mudstone or claystone.
